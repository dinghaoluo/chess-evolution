The local reading copies have consistent filenames and SHA-256 fingerprints in the [literature catalogue](../data/curated/literature_catalogue.json); downloaded inputs and technical records are also in the [source manifest](../data/curated/research_sources.json). ## chess and Go

Merim Bilalić, Mario Graf, and Nemanja Vaci, [*Computers and chess masters: The role of AI in transforming elite human performance*](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjop.12750), *British Journal of Psychology* 117(2), 585–609 (2026; first online December 2024). The September-list top twenty, top twenty juniors under twenty, and top twenty seniors over sixty-five cover 1985–2021: classical Mega Database 2023 games, moves 11–60, and Stockfish 16 at depth fifteen produce 11.6 million decisions, including opponents (pp. 591–592); GAMs and multiple change-point models place the junior jump around 1999, range 1998–2000 (pp. 592–594, 599), and the top–junior gap narrows in the 2000s (pp. 597–598). Top players gain roughly two percentage points in engine matching between 1995 and 2010, plateau in the early 2010s, and show no detectable positive neural-engine effect (pp. 596, 604). A learning ceiling is an interpretation; the authors call the evidence circumstantial and request broader skill and age comparisons (pp. 604–605). Published article and manuscript versions identified; the published article was read in full; [OSF calibration](https://osf.io/4k52p/) waits for access after maintenance.

Anthony Strittmatter, Uwe Sunde, and Dainis Zegners, [*Life cycle patterns of cognitive performance over the long run*](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2006653117), *PNAS* 117(44), 27255–27261 (2020). Their final sample contains 24,379 games and 841 players, with Stockfish 8 judging moves 10–100; zero evaluation loss defines an optimal move, so exact first-choice matching is a different measure (pp. 27259–27260); improvement steepens in the 1990s; optimal-move shares rise from about 44% for cohorts born around the 1870s to 52% after the 1970s (p. 27257); cohorts explain more of the changing age profile than calendar-period groups (p. 27258); age/period/cohort identification requires assumptions (p. 27261). Published article read; [replication data](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/DZC0MT) not retrieved.

Fabian Gaessler and Henning Piezunka, [*Training with AI: Evidence from chess computers*](https://doi.org/10.1002/smj.3512), *Strategic Management Journal* 44(11), 2724–2750 (2023). Regional access before and after the Soviet collapse supplies the exposure comparison, without individual training logs (pp. 2728–2734). Benefits occur when computers are stronger than the player (pp. 2736–2738); the incremental benefit from an opponent's blunder is smaller, interaction −0.028, SE 0.013 (Table 4, p. 2740), which concerns a learned response without measuring skill decay after withdrawing computers. Published article read.

Sukwoong Choi, Hyo Kang, Namil Kim, and Junsik Kim, [*How does artificial intelligence improve human decision-making? Evidence from the AI-powered Go program*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08704v2), arXiv:2310.08704, January 2025 v2 of a 2023 preprint. The accepted manuscript studies 24,973 games and 749,190 moves, using the first thirty plies per game (pp. 15–17). Improvement follows public AI release; larger gains occur among younger players and at lower quality quantiles, with effects strongest in early-game windows (pp. 17–24, 26–27). 'Young' means below twenty-seven at release (p. 19); individual usage is unobserved, with no unexposed control group (p. 31); the stated final count of 46,454 observations differs from the 47,292 in Table 3; v2 and its tables were read, without the separate online supplement or a reconciliation of those counts.

Sebastian Krakowski, Johannes Luger, and Sebastian Raisch, [*Artificial intelligence and the changing sources of competitive advantage*](https://doi.org/10.1002/smj.3387), *Strategic Management Journal* 44(6), 1425–1452 (2023; first online February 2022). Conventional ratings predict assisted capability poorly; the negative engine-chess association has p = 0.0632 (Table 4, p. 1442); ten of twelve traced winners had computing-related education and employment, from twenty winners examined (p. 1443, footnote 20). Prior assisted performance measures capability; the biographies are a selected descriptive supplement. Published article read.

Fabian Anicker, [*When machines take over: professional chess as a model case for the societal impact of superhuman AI*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00146-025-02465-w), *AI & Society* 41, 363–375 (2026; first online 12 July 2025). Engine authority appears on p. 367; players as students of AI on p. 368; commentary and audience roles on pp. 369–371. The streaming-income comparison is qualified and individual (p. 370); generalisation is limited by the value placed on human play for itself (pp. 372–373). The published article was read; the file named `AI_chess_v3_r2_deanonym.pdf` belongs to Bilalić, Graf, and Vaci.

Egor Lappo, Noah A. Rosenberg, and Marcus W. Feldman, [*Cultural transmission of move choice in chess*](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2023.1634), *Proceedings of the Royal Society B* 290(2011), 20231634 (2023). Models fitted to move frequencies in three positions support frequency, prestige, and success biases (pp. 8–10). Engines are suggested contributors, without a tested engine-exposure effect (p. 9), and broadcasts appear in the abstract (p. 1); copying a player's move and adopting it after engine study cannot be distinguished by these observations (p. 11). Full article read.

## workplace evidence

Erik Brynjolfsson, Danielle Li, and Lindsey Raymond, [*Generative AI at work*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11771v2), arXiv:2304.11771v2, November 2024. The 5,172-agent rollout raises issues resolved per hour by about 15%, with larger gains among less skilled workers and small quality declines at the top (pp. 16–20). Outage handling time supports learning under stated limits (pp. 25–26); problem rarity and assisted English changes are on pp. 27–30, with measurement in Appendix A, pp. 61–65; globally rarest topics do not have the largest gain: rarity to the individual and rarity across the firm differ. Full v2, including appendices, read; mechanisms and uncertainty are in [transfer](transfer.md).

Fabrizio Dell'Acqua, Edward McFowland III, Ethan R. Mollick, Hila Lifshitz-Assaf, Katherine C. Kellogg, Saran Rajendran, Lisa Krayer, François Candelon, and Karim R. Lakhani, [*Navigating the jagged technological frontier: Field experimental evidence of the effects of AI on knowledge worker productivity and quality*](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.4573321), HBS Working Paper 24-013 (2023). Inside-frontier effects are on pp. 9–11 and Tables 1–2, pp. 34–35; outside-frontier correctness on pp. 13–14 and Table 4, p. 37; centaur/cyborg observations on p. 16 and Appendix E; the 43%/17% split compares treated participants with their prior assessment. Full 58-page paper read; body pagination restarts after the HBS covers and abstract.

Nikhil Agarwal, Alex Moehring, Pranav Rajpurkar, and Tobias Salz, [*Combining human expertise with artificial intelligence: Experimental evidence from radiology*](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31422), NBER Working Paper 31422, original July 2023 version. Average diagnostic results are on pp. 23–27; underweighting and signal-dependence modelling on pp. 36–39; conditional delegation on pp. 41–44. The [original revision file](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w31422/revisions/w31422.rev0.pdf) was read in full. The local `w31422.pdf` is revised August 2026; its claims and pagination are separate from those of the July 2023 original.

Krzysztof Budzyń et al., [*Endoscopist deskilling risk after exposure to artificial intelligence in colonoscopy: a multicentre, observational study*](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-1253(25)00133-5), *Lancet Gastroenterology & Hepatology* 10(10), 896–903 (2025). The WRAP manuscript gives design and analysis on pp. 8–13, detection results on pp. 14–16, and limitations on pp. 18–19; Table 2, p. 27, gives 28.4% versus 22.4%, difference −6.0 points (95% CI −10.5 to −1.6). The before/after exposure contrast is observational despite the underlying trial's randomised AI-on/off days. Full WRAP manuscript and appended tables read; cite these manuscript pages for the local copy.

Michelle Vaccaro, Abdullah Almaatouq, and Thomas Malone, [*When combinations of humans and AI are useful: A systematic review and meta-analysis*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-024-02024-1), *Nature Human Behaviour* 8, 2293–2303 (2024). The review contains 106 experiments from 74 papers; pooled effects versus humans and versus the better partner appear on pp. 2294–2295, relative-capability moderation on p. 2296; more than 95% of experiments let humans make the final choice; separate-task delegation is rare in this evidence (p. 2297). The positive creation-task estimate has a confidence interval crossing zero; the full article was read without its separately hosted supplement.

# essay outline

The [claims ledger](claims.md) gives source pages and caveats; [chess and work](transfer.md) compares the mechanisms and their conditions.

## 1. the window

Humans added useful preparation and direction during the early freestyle period; later results show how narrow the opportunity for decisive live intervention became.

- Becca's question; Kasparov's [account of the 2005 event](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2010/02/11/the-chess-master-and-the-computer/), published in 2010: 'Weak human + machine + better process was superior to a strong computer alone.'
- ZackS against Dobrov: roughly 25 minutes saved through move seventeen, spent within six moves. At 33...Qc6, ZackS had 12m28 and Dobrov at most 12m18; shared searches favour the near-equal ...Qxb4. [C01–C02](claims.md)
- Hydra's two accounts scored 7.5/16 against the field. Zor_champ won the next edition in 2006; the Rybka author's team won editions three and six. [C03](claims.md)
- Two games classified as board results out of 272 across ICCF finals 32 and 33. Final 32 has thirteen batch resignations, one contested resignation, one input error, one mating blunder, and one board win; final 33 has ten awards following Dronov's death and 126 draws. [C04](claims.md)
- Figures: [both clocks](../plots/pal_css_2005_clock.png); [ICCF grids](../plots/iccf_outcomes.png).
- Later freestyle and ICCF results [C03–C04](claims.md) show little evidence of decisive human intervention. Across workplace experiments, combinations often fall below the stronger partner [C20](claims.md); the radiology model usually favours separate delegation [C18](claims.md).

Test the person's contribution against the machine alone with comparable resources, then retain human intervention where that comparison shows an improvement in the outcome being judged.

## 2. squeezed value

Human play improved as engines became training partners, but gains concentrated among those with more to learn; stronger human performance need not preserve an advantage over the machine.

- Bilalić's juniors improve sharply around 1999 and narrow the elite gap; Strittmatter finds cohort changes, and Choi finds larger gains among younger Go players [C05–C08](claims.md). Less skilled customer-support agents also gain more [C16](claims.md).
- No neural-engine gain is detected at the chess elite [C06](claims.md); top support agents gain little and show some quality declines [C16](claims.md). Vaccaro compares assisted performance with the stronger partner alone [C20](claims.md): improving humans need not improve the combination.
- Players trained with computers exploit human blunders less effectively [C09](claims.md); endoscopists' unassisted detection falls after AI exposure [C19](claims.md). The chess study does not measure general skill decay.

Use machine examples to learn unfamiliar decisions, and periodically check unaided performance so that faster assisted work is not mistaken for retained skill in making those decisions.

## 3. stale knowledge

Engine recommendations change across releases, and workplace AI succeeds on some tasks while failing on others; advice needs checking against the current tool and decision.

- SF4/SF17.1 agree in 52.4% of 500 positions; SF4's choice costs more than 0.02 expected score in 87, with matched release disagreement 25 points above the budget baseline (95% interval 11–39) [C13](claims.md). Consultants improve inside the tested frontier but lose nineteen points of correctness outside it [C17](claims.md).
- Nielsen's 2007 Rxb5 is unselected through tested SF12, near-equal or reversed in SF13–14, and selected at every budget from SF15 in 2022 [C14](claims.md); the consulting comparison [C17](claims.md) makes checking the particular task essential even after success elsewhere.
- Move frequencies are consistent with social transmission biases [C15](claims.md); support agents retain some handling-time gains during outages [C16](claims.md). Learning can outlast direct access to its source; neither study tests whether the learnt advice later becomes obsolete.
- Figures: [Nielsen releases](../plots/nielsen_releases.png); [release disagreement and budget sensitivity](../plots/advice_noise_floor.png).

Record which tool and version supplied important advice, then retest it when either the tool or the conditions of the decision change.

## 4. jobs and what to do

Chess retained human activity and developed roles around engine authority; workplace task gains show possible changes in work, without establishing what happens to employment.

- Chess has a fixed objective and a cheap oracle [C11](claims.md). Radiology's allocation problem [C18](claims.md) depends on error and verification costs; an engine-like check is often unavailable in work.
- Conventional ratings poorly predict assisted-chess success [C12](claims.md); consultants' centaur and cyborg workflows describe different allocations of effort [C17](claims.md).
- Changed roles [C11](claims.md) and customer-support productivity [C16](claims.md) establish neither economy-wide employment effects nor social welfare; human chess can also be valued for the activity itself.
- Headline growth depends on definition: listed 2250+ entries rise, active counts vary by threshold [C10](claims.md), which qualifies the description of the field. Supporting [participation counts](participation.md) and [figure](../plots/rating_participation.png).

Build judgement about where the machine fails, with verification before delegation; keep practising the human activities whose value does not depend on beating it.

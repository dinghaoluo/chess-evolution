# learning corpus audit

Six Lumbra batches match their archived hashes, although an OTB batch label cannot establish classical human play: their 9,084,190 records include computer, correspondence, and blitz games.

Michael Jansen's database is distributed under CC BY-NC 4.0, distinct from the separately licensed Lichess collection; the [source manifest](../data/curated/learning_corpus_sources.json) retains attribution and fingerprints; unpacked files remain local.

The [old indexer audit](../data/curated/retired_indexer_audit.json) found exclusions designed for recorded precedent, including championship-copy removal and a both-players-2200 floor; its date substitutions and sampling priorities cannot be reused for mover-band learning.

Five documented events supply 5,780 eligible games: thirteen from 1985, fifteen from 1995, forty-one from 2005, eighty-five from 2015, and 5,626 from 2024. Four events require matching full main lines, results, and compatible participant names against OlimpBase; the 1995 Pan-Pacific score source remains independently unverified. Wrong-player copies and conflicting event records are excluded; strict name matching also excludes some genuine games.

The frozen seed and one-player-year cap yield 252 of the target 750 player-games; nearest-January joins match 234 identities (92.9%) and supply 219 usable birth years (86.9%); both PGN and independent event ratings remain recorded where they conflict.

Sparse early strata, imperfect identities, and rating provenance prevent historical band or age comparisons. The corpus is capped: no further event pass is used in this round, and the pilot remains a methods result while the essay uses published learning evidence.

# hypotheses and analysis boundaries

Recorded 16 October 2026, before new corpus sampling or candidate-score results. Existing case selections, release matches, clocks, and the retired recorded-precedent results were already inspected. This is a local analysis protocol, without an independent registration or an immutable public timestamp; amendments must retain their date and reason.

## a. quality of unassisted human play

The directional hypothesis is that mean expected-score loss falls over time in classical games, with a larger decline after 2008 in a 2200–2399 population than an extrapolation of its pre-1997 trend predicts. This remains a descriptive historical comparison: neither 1997 nor 2008 assigns access to training tools, and a rating band can change composition as players improve and leave it.

- Sample player-games, with at most one sampled game per player-year before increasing the cap; use five pilot years (1985, 1995, 2005, 2015, and 2024), 50 player-games per population and year, sampled without replacement with seed 20261016. A shortage is a reported shortage. Both sides may contribute when eligible, but the game remains one uncertainty cluster.
- Define elite membership from an independently sourced January standard-rating top 100 for each year; define the comparison population by the mover's recorded standard rating, 2200–2399 inclusive. Missing identities, ratings, or classical status are exclusions with counts. Do not call the second group club players. Audit historical rating availability before finalising the start year.
- Require a verified classical event or time control, standard starting position, a legal nonempty main line, and played status. Deduplicate full main lines with event and player checks; audit conflicting dates and identities. Title matches are a separate, selected population and a throughput check only.
- Score moves 9–40 inclusive with Stockfish 17.1, one thread, fixed nodes, full history, fresh hash, and fixed options. This window reduces exposure to early book and late endings; it excludes neither preparation nor all tablebase positions. Flag positions with seven or fewer pieces and report a sensitivity excluding them.
- Compare the played move with the screening recommendation in a complete shared-root search; retain both raw signed differences and nonnegative loss, explicitly counting reversed rankings. Use the same engine's WDL expectation throughout, with version and perspective saved. Define a blunder as loss greater than 0.10. Repeat at confirmation budget on a stratified sample chosen independently of the losses.
- Record all legal alternatives for a smaller, presampled difficulty subset. Count moves within 0.05 expected score of the best and the best-to-second gap only when all roots are covered at a common completed depth. These engine-derived descriptors leave human difficulty unmeasured; a Regan intrinsic rating would require a separately fitted and calibrated model. Report missing or unstable features.
- Report player-game means first; bootstrap games and assess repeated-player dependence. Report colour, opponent rating, opening, event, game length, and available time-control sensitivity. Later-move windows (16–40 and 21–40) are prespecified preparation sensitivities. Position adjustment can remove an effect mediated through opening choices, so retain both adjusted and unadjusted comparisons.

An unchanged or increasing loss, a post-2008 change consistent with the earlier trend, or a sign that reverses under budget and coverage checks counts against the hypothesis. A noisy pilot is inconclusive; before the full run, choose a minimum detectable effect and sample size from pilot variance, then freeze them in a dated amendment. Milestones are annotations, without searching for a favourable breakpoint. Published chess studies already bear on this question; their evidence belongs in the essay even if the new corpus cannot support this test.

## b. who gained

The compression hypothesis is that the difference in mean loss between the two populations narrows over time; it is contradicted by a stable or widening gap under common sampling and budget rules. Estimate the difference in changes from A, with game and repeated-player uncertainty, and distinguish cohort replacement from within-player improvement wherever identities permit. Elite status and a fixed rating band answer different selection questions, so report the membership turnover too.

Historical rating spread is a separate descriptive series: use January lists, standard ratings, a fixed threshold, active status where available, and documented list definitions. Do not compare changing whole-list medians. FIDE's March 2024 compression and floor change require a separate boundary treatment; a narrower rating distribution cannot establish a narrower move-quality distribution.

## c. adoption of a proposed neural-engine pattern

The adoption hypothesis is that an early attacking h-pawn advance becomes more frequent after the publication of neural-engine examples. Freeze the detector before counting: a white h-pawn first reaches h5 or h6, or a black h-pawn first reaches h4 or h3, by full move 24; the opponent must have previously castled kingside and still have a king on the g- or h-file. Require queens and at least four total rooks, bishops, or knights on the board. Count one qualifying event per side-game; divide by side-games with an opponent castled kingside by move 24, and report the stricter set with a qualifying material-and-king opportunity.

Use the same eligibility, years, and sampling across populations. For the comparison pattern, apply the same rule to the original a-pawn reaching a5 or a6 for White, or a4 or a3 for Black, with the same kingside-castling, material, and move limits; it may also respond to engine teaching. Unit fixtures must cover colour symmetry, captures onto the h-file, absent queens, queenside castling, and the move-25 boundary. Track the original h-file pawn through captures so that another pawn reaching h5 cannot qualify.

No increase, a similar change in the comparison pattern, or an increase preceding the proposed exposure counts against the timing hypothesis. Yearly frequencies and elite membership do not identify access to explanations or establish a difference-in-differences treatment; test pretrends descriptively, without interpreting the comparison as causal. A change in style can occur without a change in decision quality. Evidence for learning from explanations must come from a design that measures explanation access.

## d. revision of engine preferences and opening use

The preference hypothesis is that exact first-choice agreement falls with greater separation between sampled releases. Sample up to 2,000 positions across fixed year, population, move, and material strata before any historical searches; retain one sampled position per game, complete history, all budgets, and the reference list. Score the preferred move of each release under a common modern judge, with near-equal alternatives retained. Distinguish preference changes from changes exceeding 0.02 and 0.05 expected score. Release pairs reuse positions, so resample at game level.

A flat or non-monotone relationship, or disagreement confined to near-equal alternatives, counts against a simple decay account. A new engine preference is an estimated preference, without ground-truth proof that the old advice was false; sparse releases cannot identify a knowledge half-life. State unequal node costs and the modern judge's own search uncertainty.

The opening-turnover hypothesis is that the probability of seeing a ply-16 move sequence again five years later declines in later eras. Draw equal game counts independently for each year, count unique sequences with their frequency, require complete five-year follow-up (last origin year 2019 for a corpus ending in 2024), and repeat the sampling. Report position-based transposition sensitivity separately. No decline, or a decline removed by sample-size and event-mix controls, counts against faster turnover. Non-observation in the sample does not establish refutation or abandonment.

## e. human participation after superhuman engines

The persistence hypothesis is that human participation above a common observable standard-rating threshold did not collapse after the late 1990s. Keep rated-list membership, active membership, title holders, and annual title awards separate, with list dates and eligibility changes attached. Use the highest historical floor in the chosen period as the minimum common threshold, or shorten the period; do not assume 2200 covers every historical list. Publish an interrupted series where definitions cannot be reconciled.

A sustained fall under comparable definitions counts against persistence; growth cannot establish engine causation, professional income, or security of employment. Title counts accumulate and titles have changing award conditions. Counts above 2700 require rating-pool context, and an old no-inflation estimate cannot certify later lists. Human competition has value as human competition; this limits any inference to work whose buyers value the output independently of its maker.

## collaboration cases and optional extension

The eighteen existing decisions are participant-selected cases, with opponents' moves and retrospective claims included. Preserve those categories. For each engine and budget, distinguish selecting the move, an absolute finite candidate-score gap within 20 centipawns, a larger estimated rejection, and a search reversal in which the forced played move scores better. Repeat the classification at 10 and 30 centipawns; do not compare centipawn magnitudes across releases. Mate results need their own ordering. Fruit's ignored root restriction requires an explicit unavailable result until validated.

Agreement across all three budgets is a stability description, without a test of human contribution against equal hardware and preparation. The preparation-versus-play comparison is exploratory because the cases and hypothesis were chosen after the examples were known. Maia-based allocation remains optional until the main comparisons work; its predictions would require validation against observed human choices.

### variance-pilot search settings, 16 October 2026

Reason: newly read literature, 16 October 2026. The source audit found rapid, correspondence, and computer games within the OTB-labelled batches, so an initial pilot must use documented classical events and retain shortages. This restricted pool supplies event-conditional variance and throughput only; it cannot establish historical band gaps or a defensible sample size for the broader corpus.

Use 25,000 nodes for an unrestricted single-PV recommendation, followed by 25,000 nodes shared between the recommendation and played move when they differ. Retain the last complete equal-depth MultiPV cycle, full history, native WDL, signed differences, and actual searched nodes. A matching played move has zero measured loss against this recommendation. Select one position per sampled game by a seed-based hash before searches, and repeat its screening and paired comparison at 100,000 nodes. These are pilot budgets; main budgets remain unfrozen until stability and throughput are inspected.

For the secondary window, average the per-move Lichess accuracy transformation within a player-game; do not call this Lichess's volatility-weighted whole-game accuracy. Apply the published win-percentage and move-accuracy formulae to the same-board paired candidate evaluations. Clamp negative win-percentage deterioration to zero and accuracy to 0–100. Retain mate scores separately; use signed 100,000-centipawn proxies only for the accuracy transformation, and exclude mate comparisons from centipawn error denominators. Report cumulative nonnegative centipawn losses of at least 50, 100, and 200, with mutually exclusive 50–<100, 100–<200, and 200+ bins as additional columns. Exact boundary conventions are explicit because the published method and figure captions use different descriptions. These secondary outputs permit qualitative comparison until the authors' inputs and aggregation can be checked.
